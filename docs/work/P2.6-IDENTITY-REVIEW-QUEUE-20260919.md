Completed **P2.6 — Identity review queue**. The review queue is now first-class:

review queue (the `DECISION_SUPERSEDE_UNKNOWN` review_id collision) that had been
spec'd as a future spec but never landed in code.

## The defect (review_id collision / data loss)

When a `supersede` decision references an unknown `supersedes_decision_id`, the
compiler emits a `DECISION_SUPERSEDE_UNKNOWN` review. Because a supersede decision
references another *decision* (not observations), the review was created with
`affected_observation_ids: []`. `_review` derived the `review_id` from
`stable_id("rev", code, ids)` where `ids` was the **empty** observation list, so two
distinct supersede-unknown reviews (different unknown targets) collapsed to the
**same** `review_id`. `compile_identity` stores reviews in `review_by_id` (a dict
keyed by `review_id`), so the first review was silently dropped.

Reproduced on `main` (`14c8a0e`) with two supersede decisions referencing two
different unknown decisions:

```text
review_count: 1
distinct review_ids: 1   # the dec-sup1 -> dec-ghost-a review was lost
```

## What landed

- `ledger/identity.py`:
  - `_review` now accepts `decision_ids`, `severity`, `reason_automation_stopped`,
    and `resolution_actions`, and emits a first-class envelope with
    `affected_decision_ids` and those fields.
  - The decision referent is fed into the `stable_id` material **only when present**,
    so two distinct decision-scoped reviews get distinct `review_id`s while
    observation-scoped review IDs are unchanged (pinned fixtures preserved).
  - Every review emission (`PROJECT_KEY_AMBIGUOUS`, `AUTO_MATCH_BLOCKED_BY_DECISION`,
    `DECISION_REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `DECISION_CONFLICT`,
    `DECISION_SUPERSEDE_UNKNOWN`) now carries severity, reason automation stopped,
    and resolution actions. `DECISION_CONFLICT` is `error` severity; the rest are
    `warning`.
- `tests/test_p26_review_queue.py` — 5 tests covering: the first-class envelope,
  error severity for decision conflicts, the decision-scoped referent, the
  distinct-review_id regression (no collision), and that observation-scoped review
  IDs are unchanged.
